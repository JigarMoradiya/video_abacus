Meet the Abacus

What an abacus is, and the name of every part

shipped 72 lines 4:19 as scripted

1 · HOOK

1Can your child count all the way to one hundred, but ask, what's seven plus eight?
2Out come the fingers!
3There's a little step missing, and this is the tool that helps.
4This is an abacus.
5By the end of this video, you'll know every part of it.

2 · WHAT AN ABACUS IS

6An abacus is a counting tool.
7It has colorful beads that slide up and down on rods.
8People used abacuses to solve maths problems long before calculators were invented.

3 · THE OUTSIDE: FRAME, RODS, BEAM

9Let's look at the outside first.
10This outside part is called the frame.
11It holds everything together.
12These long lines are called rods.
13Some people also call them columns.
14They mean the same thing.
15Right across the middle is the beam.
16Some people call it the bar.

4 · THE TWO HALVES

17The beam is very important.
18It splits the abacus into two parts.
19Everything above the beam is the top section.
20Everything below the beam is the bottom section.
21Look at the top.
22Each rod has one upper bead.
23Now look at the bottom.
24Each rod has four lower beads.
25Sometimes people call them heaven beads and earth beads.
26But don't worry, upper and lower are the names we'll use.

5 · WHERE TO START

27Now look at the rod on the far right.
28This is the ones column.
29This is where we always start.

6 · WHAT EACH BEAD IS WORTH

30Each lower bead is worth one.
31The upper bead is worth five.
32One little bead, worth five.
33That's the clever part of an abacus.
34Now move one rod to the left.
35Everything becomes ten times bigger.
36Each lower bead is now worth ten.
37The upper bead is worth fifty.
38Move one more rod left.
39Now each lower bead is worth one hundred.
40The upper bead is worth five hundred.
41Every time you move left, the value becomes ten times bigger.

7 · HOW MUCH IT HOLDS

42So how many numbers can we make?
43One rod can show any number from zero to nine.
44Two rods can show zero to ninety-nine.
45Three rods can show zero to nine hundred and ninety-nine.
46And bigger abacuses can show even bigger numbers.

7a · BIG ABACUS, SMALL ABACUS

47Here's something interesting.
48The abacus in our app has thirteen rods.
49On a big abacus like that, the ones column is in the middle.
50The rods on the right are used for decimals.
51But on a small abacus like this one, we simply start on the far right.

8 · THE TWO FINGERS

52Now let's learn the finger trick.
53You only need two fingers.
54Your thumb and your index finger.
55To add lower beads, push them up with your thumb.
56To add the upper bead, pull it down with your index finger.
57To subtract lower beads, push them down with your index finger.
58To subtract the upper bead, push it back up with your thumb.
59Just two fingers, four easy rules.
60That's it.

9 · READ YOUR FIRST NUMBER

61Let's read an abacus together.
62The upper bead is down. That's five.
63Three lower beads are up. That's three more.
64Five plus three. Eight.

10 · YOUR TURN

65Your turn. What number is this showing?
66It's seven. One upper bead and two lower beads.
67Great job.

Adding Two Numbers

3When we put two numbers together, that's called adding.

2 · WHAT ADDING MEANS

5Adding on the abacus means moving more beads to the beam.
6First, make your starting number.
7Then, push up as many more beads as you want to add.
8When you stop, the rod is already showing the answer.

3 · ONE PLUS TWO

9Let's try one plus two together.
10To make one, push one lower bead up to the beam.
11The rod is showing one.
12Now, add two by pushing two more lower beads up.
13Count them as they go up.
14One, two. Three lower beads are touching the beam.
15So, one plus two is three.

4 · TWO PLUS TWO

16Now, let's try two plus two.
17To make two, push two lower beads up to the beam.
18Now, add two more by pushing two more lower beads up.
19All four lower beads are touching the beam.
20So, two plus two is four.

5 · THE LOWER-BEAD RULE

21Every lower bead you push up adds one more.
22But each rod only has four lower beads.
23So, the lower beads can only make one, two, three or four.

6 · THE UPPER BEAD ADDS FIVE

24When we want to add five, we use the upper bead instead.
25Start with all the beads away from the beam.
26So, the rod is showing zero.
27Now, push the upper bead down until it touches the beam.
28The rod is showing five.
29One single bead gave us five in one move.

7 · FIVE PLUS ONE

30Now, let's try five plus one.

8 · FIVE PLUS FOUR

Bigger Numbers

Place value: the tens rod, the hundreds rod, and reading both

1 · HOOK

1Last time, we added two numbers on one rod.
2But one rod can only show up to nine.
3So what do we do when a number is bigger than nine?
4That is what the other rods are for.

2 · WHICH ROD IS WHICH

5On an abacus this size, the ones rod is the one on the far right.
6On a much bigger abacus, the ones rod sits in the middle instead — we saw that in the first video.
7Now look at the rod just to the left of the ones rod.
8That one is the tens rod.
9Every bead on the tens rod is worth ten, not one.

3 · MAKING TEN

10Let's make ten.
11Push one lower bead up on the tens rod.
12Leave the ones rod at zero.
13One bead on the tens rod is one ten, and one ten is ten.

4 · TWENTY-THREE

14Now let's make twenty-three.
15On the tens rod, push two lower beads up.
16Two beads on the tens rod is two tens, which is twenty.
17Now, on the ones rod, push three lower beads up.
18That is three ones.
19Twenty and three is twenty-three.

5 · THE READING RULE

20Here is how to read any number on two rods.
21Read the tens rod first, then read the ones rod.

6 · FIFTY-SIX

22Let's try fifty-six.
23On the tens rod, push the upper bead down.
24The upper bead on the tens rod is worth five tens, which is fifty.
25On the ones rod, push the upper bead down for five.
26Then push one lower bead up for one.
27That makes six ones.
28Fifty and six is fifty-six.

7 · NINETY-NINE, THE MAXIMUM

29What is the biggest number that two rods can show?
30On the tens rod, push the upper bead down and all four lower beads up.
31That is nine tens, which is ninety.
32Now do exactly the same on the ones rod.
33That is nine ones.
34Ninety and nine is ninety-nine.
35Ninety-nine is the most that two rods can show.
36So with two rods, you can make every number from zero to ninety-nine.
37That is one hundred different numbers.

38Now it is your turn.
