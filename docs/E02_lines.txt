# --- 1 · HOOK ---
Last time, we met the abacus.
Today we make numbers with it.
Every number from zero all the way up to nine.
And you only need one rod.

# --- 2 · ZERO ---
Let's start with nothing at all. Zero.
Zero means no beads are touching the beam.
Push them all away, and the rod says zero.

# --- 3 · ONE TO FOUR ---
# Count the runs clearly and evenly — the aligner needs to hear each number separately.
Now push one lower bead up to the beam.
That is one.
Push one more. Count them. One, two.
That is two.
One more again. One, two, three.
That is three.
And one more. One, two, three, four.
That is four.

# --- 4 · THE WALL ---
# Line 17: a little puzzled on "but there are none left". This is the problem beat.
Now let's try to make five.
Push another lower bead up... but there are none left.
Four is as high as the lower beads can go.
So how do we make five?

# --- 5 · THE UPPER BEAD IS FIVE ---
# Line 20 is the reveal. Slow down and let it arrive.
Look above the beam. The upper bead.
It has been waiting up there the whole time.
Send the lower beads back down...
...and bring the upper bead down with your index finger.
One upper bead, and the rod says five.
That single bead is worth five all on its own.

# --- 6 · SIX TO NINE ---
Now five is easy to build on.
Five, and one lower bead up. That is six.
Five, and two lower beads. Seven.
Five, and three. Eight.
Five, and all four. Nine.
Nine is the biggest number one rod can show.

# --- 7 · THE READING RULE ---
Reading a rod is always the same two steps.
Is the upper bead down? Then that is five.
Then count the lower beads touching the beam.
Add them together, and that is your number.

# --- 8 · YOUR TURN ---
# HOLD THREE FULL SECONDS OF SILENCE after the next line. Do not cut it short.
Your turn. What number is this rod showing?
It is three. Three lower beads up, and the upper bead still resting.
Nice work.
