# TEACH, DO NOT READ. A narrator states facts in a row; a teacher does four things a narrator
# never does, and all four are built into the lines below:
#
#   1. ASKS, then WAITS. Every ⏸ is a real silence. Do not fill it. The child is answering.
#      Then say their answer back to them as if you heard it — "Eight! Well done."
#   2. REACTS. "Oh! Zero already?" is not information, it is a face. Play the surprise, then
#      the reveal.
#   3. TALKS TO ONE CHILD. "You tell me", "your abacus", "you are getting quick at this."
#      Never "we can observe that". One child, sitting next to you.
#   4. CHANGES SPEED. Slow and careful on a new rule. Quick and light on "Done!" and "Friends!"

# The ⏸ marks are part of the script. If they are read straight through, the episode becomes a
# lecture again no matter how warmly the lines are said.

# ═══ CONTENT ═══
#
# E11 did NOT teach only one pair — it fully worked THREE (9+1, 8+2, 7+3, all bead-by-bead). An
# earlier version of this note said otherwise; it was wrong, and it led the first draft of this
# script to re-teach 8+2 and 7+3 bead-for-bead a second time, which is just repeated footage with
# new numbers stapled on. Fixed: E12 now only re-teaches what is actually new. That leaves exactly
# TWO pairs E11 never touched — four and six, five and five — and those are where this episode's
# real teaching time goes. The other three get named and recapped, not re-worked.
#
# TWO RODS, LIVE, FROM FRAME 0 — same as E11. Every worked sum here crosses ten, so the tens rod
# is doing real work the whole episode, not sitting there for "bigger numbers".
#
# BEAD ORDER — the same rule E11 corrected to, and this episode must not drift from it:
#   - RODS, RIGHT TO LEFT: the ones rod is always dealt with FIRST (build it, then take the
#     friend away from it), and only THEN does the tens rod get its one carried bead. Never the
#     other way round.
#   - ON A ROD, BOTTOM FIRST: lower (earth) beads move before the upper (heaven) bead — true
#     whether beads are going ON (building a number) or OFF (taking the friend away). This is
#     NOT reversed from Small Friend; it is the exact same rule, with the carry added as one
#     extra step at the end.
#
# THE WORKED SUMS, every one checked digit by digit:
#   6 + 2 = 8   DIRECT — plenty of room on the ones rod, no trick, this is the point of section 3
#   9 + 1 = 10  NAMED ONLY — E11's own pair, called back to by name, not re-worked
#   8 + 2 = 10  NAMED ONLY — already fully worked in E11, not repeated here
#   7 + 3 = 10  NAMED ONLY — already fully worked in E11, not repeated here
#   2 + 8 = 10  friend 2   take away two, then carry                WE SHOW (flip of 8+2, section 4)
#   6 + 4 = 10  friend 6   take away six, then carry                WE SHOW (new, full detail)
#   4 + 6 = 10  friend 4   take away four, then carry                WE SHOW (flip of 6+4, section 6a)
#   5 + 5 = 10  friend 5   take away five, then carry               THEY DO (new)
#
# ALL FIVE PAIRS ARE NAMED in section 2. Note there are really NINE directional formulas here, not
# five — `+n = −friend+10` for n=1..9 are nine different operations (different starting rod value,
# different friend), since e.g. `+1` (start at 9) and `+9` (start at 1) both use the pair {1,9} but
# are not the same move. The worked examples teach the "+1..+5" direction bead-by-bead and the
# reverse direction with TWO concrete flipped examples (2+8 in section 4, 4+6 in section 6a).
# The FORMULA CARD lists ALL NINE (user call, 2026-09-23): on the pattern recap ("That is all. The
# friend changes. But ten never does.") and on the closing card, the on-screen list is
# +1 = −9+10 … +9 = −1+10, because "the friend changes but ten never does" is a statement about
# the whole set, and a five-row card there would silently claim +6..+9 do not exist. The single
# lit strip beside each worked sum is unchanged (only that sum's own formula). E11's own five-row
# card is untouched — it only ever claimed the five it taught.
#
# 5+5 is the your-turn on purpose: it is the one pair where a number is its OWN friend, a genuine
# "wait, what?" moment, and the best possible note to end the child's own turn on.
#
# NOTATION: `+n = −friend + 10` — friend first, then ten. Matches E11 exactly (do not let this
# drift back to "+10 − friend", which was the error E11 itself had to correct).
#
# ⏸ HOLD 3 SECONDS after "What number is showing now?" in section 7 — the your-turn recall gap,
# same convention every episode with a your-turn uses.

# --- 3 · WHEN DO WE NEED IT? ---
Now, careful here. This part is important.
We do not always need the big friend.
Always look at the whole rod first.
If there is still room, just push the beads.
Watch. Six plus two.
Push one lower bead up, then the upper bead down. That makes six.
Now push two more lower beads.
Done!
That was easy. No trick at all. The rod still had room.
But do you remember nine plus one?
We pushed, and pushed... and there was nothing left.
Not one bead, anywhere on the rod.
And that is when we call a big friend.


# --- 4 · YOU ALREADY KNOW THREE ---
Now, you already met three of these friends last time.
Nine and one.
Eight and two.
Seven and three.
Take away the friend, then carry to the tens rod. Every one of those made ten.

And here is something neat. It works flipped around too.
Two and eight. Make two on your abacus. Just two lower beads.
Eight's friend is two — and that is exactly what is already there.
Take away two, then carry one bead to the tens rod.
Two plus eight is ten. Same trick, just flipped.

So today, let us learn the last two.


# --- 5 · ADD FOUR ---
Four's friend is six.
Let us make six. Push one lower bead up, then the upper bead down.
Now we want to add four.
Remember our rule. The ones rod goes first, and lower beads move before the upper bead.
So take away six. Down comes one lower bead, then up goes the upper bead.
Now the ones rod shows zero.
Oh! Zero already?
Do not worry. Watch what happens next.
Now the tens rod gets its turn. Carry one bead.
And there it is. Ten!
Six plus four is ten.
We wanted four more, and we got it.
